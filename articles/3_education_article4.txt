**Council Rides on a ’Zero‑Debt’ Commitment for Port Augusta Public Schools**  

A formal declaration entered the municipal clerk’s rolls this week in an attempt to reassure residents that educational expenditure should not burden taxpayers during the next eight years. Mayor Laura Ng Suh, sitting beneath Port Augusta High Street and flattered eyes over the city hall dais on Wednesday dawn, told reporters that the $1 5m per annum annual council levy will support **"a Zero Debt pathway" for every local public junior — senior college under council control."**

“Kids are paying on the next generation of jobs, not on legacy budgets," Ms S’s emphatic slogan hammered at an event attended by two hundred school children’s carers and headmasters John Barham from Augusta Primary, Hannah Doherty at Port Alderson School.  

Council members will keep debts zeroed via an **allocation method where any residual levied revenue will be used immediately, leaving no ‘cousin funds’ open for later years where taxpayers take on more debt**. The measure will, as declared, see a direct lift, "Zero Debt, More Cash," to classrooms to fund teacher salary premiums in next round at +14% — the most dramatic across council-managed, and not funded by State contributions.


### Heads across schools

Hugh McGon‑Hunt – Principal Port’s **Port Alderson Public College** wrote a letter to local community, echoing the sentiment that “this Council promise will, according to research & my 12-year audit oversight in Australian federal policy frameworks, ensure no longer-than a month budget debt, thereby strengthening the safety net of students' education quality.”

She added concern: “There isn’t enough funding transparency though we cannot deny this is good enough policy on time until $15m for the schools.  
We see a 75% reduction expected in debt and the 3 per mil growth to next academic fiscal timeline”  

In reply, the school board and PTA in **East Port Augusta Primary School** are working on extra resources, hoping zero‑debt might let $500, the same sum once earmarked for repairs from building 1967 now move free 200 per cent of capital spending over to actual training. Their teachers also “hopes that there will remain flexibility to upgrade high performance for staff.”

### Opposition notes

While teachers applaud the strategy to keep schools under debt, some community advocacy, particularly from senior citizen club "Golden‑Years", say there's not yet detailed accounting on cash buffer, calling this still "policy" "but a financial approach with ‘zero debt” risk.

"It sounds nice on paper and may deliver real benefit – but what in it is truly needed that we can watch for?" warns Mark Phillips CEO “Northern Schools Improvement Partners.”

Counsillor Tim White also expressed a degree of warning at his position paper on "debt transparency requirements"

> “That a simple 70-year period might leave open ‘unexpected growth rates and revenue dips if future schools might have capital projects that do require credit. In these high-savings models our key interest needs to monitor 'not the zero but actual growth', else the board might appear not that transparent or, if miscalculated, this will still fall for debt later" – concluded by a subtle apology of their office on 'no-press, no-debt – so the rest of us will learn to get with them.
