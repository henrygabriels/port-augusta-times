**The Port Augusta Times** 7 Nov 2024

***Port Augusta Senior Schools Get Early‑Award From Education SA For Climate‑Conscious Curriculum***

Port. This dawn will not only mark another season of sunburnt heat but also another triumphant stride for our own children– an earlier reward for the Port Augusta Secondary educational precincts. Teachers and pupils from the Peninsula North & North Adelaide Schools received an early accreditation award from the Government of South—Education Department for successfully meeting the new State Benchmark for incorporating climate‑focused STEM (C‑STEM) and community response into primary curriculum planning by mid‑October.

Why ‘early‐award’? Because the Education Ministers want every senior local teacher to see a certification within one year rather then five to qualify, and the schools submitted a joint plan already and were recognised the past evening (20 Nov during lunch service near the Harbourview complex) as the first North–East coastal region.

**Rising stars** – “After six seasons’ training,” Dr. Elaine‑Nol, principal for Al Jungar‑Port Aug: "we felt we simply couldn’t be satisfied with just making changes, only an award will cement the progress."

Curric plan includes a three‑hour series: “Ocean – Ecosystem Dynamics”; “Urban Green‑Footprints; ” and a full community garden design in School Hallways & local council‑funded rooftop compost units with ‘Solar panel‑Powered Heat‑to‑Gum‑Charger’.

Education Commissioner Matthew Benn’s comment about the early award was “These students and teachers are a shining testifier. For some our entire city would rather stay on a stable old platform; I truly respect those pushing for the first local green curriculum."

The curriculum plan will continue to receive a funding push. The school community gave evidence to support the policy decision behind the early acknowledgement by a petition containing thirty local names that are signed by parents, business and government officials with small coffee stalls across South‑Artes. They said climate is here – just as local storms. We can only adapt to make our community safe by putting lessons about sustainable water in.

"Everyone that visits these Schools knows they are actively dealing with the planet– in a practical hands-on manner," explains School Senior Adviser Mark Forde to the public for “The Daily Port”, "we teach our kids not always to survive the changing climate‐but also the how to sustain the next step for community".

We now stand at early 2055‑10 that is already an important milestone. These early award points, “Punctuate our local leadership at education and local government when it’s concerning sustainable action that matters. 

PRA’s final release continues a call for “all the state students’ will now know climate in a fun & real approach. There’ll a ‘rewards for those with an excellent demonstration plan’, but we remain, proudly waiting for more local achievements.
