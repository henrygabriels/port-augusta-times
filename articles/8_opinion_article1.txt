*By Amanda Clarke, Senior Reporter*    

Port Albert (26 Aug 20XX):  

With each grain-sheaped wave that kisses the sands off Port Augusta today, one local debate swells faster — the pace and impact of Port Albert’s proposed shift to high‑energy, climate‑optimised freight services, part and parcel of SouthAustralia’s “Sail‑Secure” transport roadmap rolled out last weekend. While municipal leadership cheers this upgrade, a clutch of experts warns that the economic surge could grind slower, smaller cargo fleets straight along the region’s shore‑ejected corridors… “It's less, not more!” 

---

#### • The Proposed ‘Smaller’ Port?  What It Means in Concrete Terms

Government releases speak vaguely over technical jargon about an **improved dock system that ‘uses energy more optimally so each cargo movement produces zero excess emissions’.** The actual result is a leaner rail and bulk haul strategy with longer vessel runs, fewer truck‑hauliers along the BHP rail, a focus on carbon‑clean fuel for rail engines and solar power stations straddling the wharf floor.

Slight but steady, the changes intend to limit traffic to:

* **Upgarde the “Big” port cranes so weight-shifting is maximised at a lean cost of twenty‑odd ton loads; 
* Install photovoltaic canopies on rail yard parking;
* Retrofit diesel freight rail to green electrics; 
     
All as part of the “Green Tugs and Paddrellas Initiative”. The projected carbon offset has been **couched at some $2M annual reduction in local emissions**
