**Small‑town Heritage DayTurns Into Tax Question That Hunts Low Income Housing Funding **  

*“Worsented” in community circles as debate sways* 

October 17, 24 — Residents and visitors took centre‑stage at the third‑year Port Augusta Community Heritage Festival, a long‑standing summer showcase meant to give tourists glimpses of the town’s unique stories. The one‑four‑day celebration opened on a sunny Sunday, featuring market stalls of bush leather art, native garden walks and speeches from longtime community voices.  
But toward Sunday’s “Local Crafts” parade the day shifted. As local produce stalls finished on a heavy cart of pumpkins—just in time to light lanterns in the main street—voices turned as the crowd’s energy waned to something much different – an all‑important headline about an upcoming tax law in the Legislative Assembly.  

The matter? An unexpectedly strong State‑government levy that, via an earmark reversal, will slash $2500 k in annual support from the province’s Low‑Income Housing Fund that feeds subsidised rentals across Port Adelaide. It wasn’t clear exactly how the cut would be carried into port. One estimate (not yet vetted by Council auditors) predicted the levy would strip half a percentage point in the tax of rental income tax on all private developments.

On the town's 30th main street line‑of-lorry from the heritage walk, Councillors stood on the footpath and explained the law’s purpose. 
> ***Council Lead Phil Carrow says** “—they hope this increase generates enough revenue to fund heritage festivals from *today’s budget*, but a short fall now for our housing board means rent hikes can reach the lowest earnest of our suburbs.*

Many locals heard their hopes evaporate there. Former community hall manager Lily Jones says: “Before there was money and now, how are homes left alone?”

On Wednesday council members announced the move from a routine annual town review to a meeting dedicated to how a change of money funding would leave many families looking into alternative “unstructured” accommodations. “We need some new ideas.”

Several community pillars who raised local business grants in the last few summer’s events (copper and wool, local music projects in the church hall and the newly‑renormalized open–air markets for locally grown tomatoes in old storage cells ) signaled concern in public meeting. Many turned into small “murmur clusters” that will take effect next February and possibly force residents reliant for monthly subsidies who can only hold the low rates to change their finances that summer months (they fear high cost of living.)

“We’ll be sure it stops at no major tax‑question’s loss,*” warned John Allen. “For now they seem to focus our best‑investment on heritage’s new brand – just keep these lines out and leave all the rest to government and our town people.”
