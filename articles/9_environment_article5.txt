**Mangrove Belt Protection Plans Pass Regional Vote**

*By Amanda Rhodes – Desk*

— **Port Augusta** – After careful debate and an all‑county ballot, a significant conservation measure will protect a corridor of riverine mangroves now threatened by sea level rise, sand transport engineering and private beach reclamation. On Monday, voters in nine electorate ridings handed the "Mangrove Belt Development Initiative" the majority‑win that passed, and town organisers called an emergency celebration at East Wallaroo’s foreshore a moment too long awaited.

**The “Mang-Buff” proposal**

Under the proposal – which councilmember Brian Ladd called a win for future–the city will extend three stretches of interspersible coastal mangrophy that currently lie dormant and under-guard‑ring: the Swan Trestfall mouth at the Gawler rivers end of the West Coast, along The Long Point out and through the Kimbles Crossing corridor near Cooleya Bay. The state government will bankroll, in partnership with non‑profits of The Australian Council Marine Institute (ACMI), a 6m wide corridor – the "Mang‑Buff".

“A lot of children of this coastal area come here to feed birds while having picnic under sun’s down‑to-earth, and our goal is to double it after all the development” said Shannna McKlerkin‑Brown—organised head of Project Eel–an eco‑tourism club and an alumna whose grand‑project involves guiding 10,000,000 a‑speak tours.
